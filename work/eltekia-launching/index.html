Beautiful

in a Deck

How El Tekia launched as the first homegrown Egyptian card game with the ambition of becoming a household name — by selling belonging, not mechanics.

يا تكسب يا تخسر اللي معاك
01 · Problem

Egypt has thousands
of card games.
None of them feel
Egyptian.

The decks on every Egyptian table — UNO, Skrew, Monopoly Deal — arrived from somewhere else. El Tekia launched as the first Egyptian-born card game with the ambition of becoming a household name in a category trained on imports. The product was ready. The question was whether anyone would pick it up over the deck their cousin already owned.

★ ★ ★
02 · Challenge

How can a brand-new card game earn a spot at the table on its first invitation?

03 · Research Findings

The real reason
people play games
isn't the game.

We started where the deck did: with a simple question — why did anyone play PUBG, Pokémon, or Skrew? The honest answer wasn't fun. It was that everyone else did.

Games don't sell themselves on mechanics; they spread because they're a way to belong. The reframe followed: El Tekia couldn't be marketed as a better card game, because better card games don't win. It had to be marketed as a way to belong — a shared chaos people would only get if they had the deck.

04 · Target Audience
The Group Chat
That Plans the Hangout

Gen Z and Millennial Egyptians, 17–28, who decide what gets played at the next ahwa night, the next Sahel weekend, the next family iftar. The trendsetters whose adoption pulls everyone else in — the same dynamic that turned PUBG and Skrew into rituals.

Behind the Insight
  • Listened to how friends actually describe their best game nights — and noticed they never describe winning. They describe the betrayal, the shouting, the photo someone took.
  • Sat in board game cafés in Cairo and Alexandria (Monoplay, UNO Board Games Cafe) to watch which decks got pulled off the shelf first and why.
  • Mapped Egyptian "belonging brands" beyond gaming — Koffee Kulture, In Your Shoe, Gazef — and traced what they sell beneath the product. The pattern was consistent: they sell a way to be inside something.
  • Read every comment under existing Egyptian card-game posts to find the moments fans quote, screenshot, and forward.

05 · Insight

The true joy
of a card game

06 · Strategy

Position El Tekia
as a game of
beautiful chaos.

A deck built to make you laugh, scheme, and bond like never before. Every Reel, every collab, every café night would dramatize the mess of playing it, not the mechanics. The product wasn't the deck. The product was the night you'd remember.

07 · The Project

A launch built around
real moments of chaos —
and a fictional Egyptian universe
woven through every piece of it.

The hero content was always going to be the players themselves: what their faces did mid-game, what their hands did under the table, what they said when they got betrayed. But raw player content alone wouldn't carry a brand. So we built a parallel illustrated Tekia universe — a cast of Egyptian archetypes set in old-Cairo backdrops — and threaded that universe through every other pillar, so that real game nights, classic-film memes, and the lore content all shared the same recognizable visual language.

01
Hero Content

Moments
of Chaos

The launch wave was anchored to real-player content captured in cafés and homes — the laugh-cry face, the suspicious squint, the moment a friend folds a card under the table. This is what the strategy promised and what the audience actually wanted to see: themselves. Crucially, every photo and video lived inside the broader Tekia world by sitting illustrated characters from the universe alongside the real players in the same frame, so the brand and the player became part of the same story.

02
Origin Moment

Cinematic Lore —
One World, Many Eras

The universe needed an origin moment. A single cinematic Reel introduced El Tekia not as a product but as a story — "حكاية بتجمع بين عصور كتير مش بس في مكان واحد لا كمان على لعبة واحدة" — an old-Cairo neighborhood frozen in animation, a slipper hovering mid-air, a portrait watching from the wall. It set the visual law everything else would follow.

Reel → التكية — حكاية بتجمع بين عصور كتير
03
Universe Cast

Character Building —
A Cast People
Could Quote

Each Tekia character got their own dedicated Reel, treated like a sitcom intro. The matriarch grandmother. The mustached patriarch with his shisha. The Kings gang. Cyber-Hammo with his music taste. The redhead summer girl. Standalone character moments turned the universe from a backdrop into an ensemble — characters fans could recognize, name, and bring up the way you'd quote a line from a Ramadan show.

04
Cultural Hijack

Memes — Egyptian Cinema
Inside the Tekia World

The strategy's meme pillar called for chaos-driven, screenshot-ready content. We took beloved scenes from classic Egyptian comedies and inlaid Tekia cards into them, captioning the clips with universally recognizable game-night feelings: بعد ما طلعلي كارت رايح جي / ترتيب القوى / لما يطلعلك كارت نقاوة. Even satirical executions like "بطولة التكية للقفز العالي" — a fake Olympics-style Tekia tournament — lived under this pillar, extending the chaos voice into ongoing absurdism.

05
Onboarding

Educational Content —
How to Play

A new game still has to teach itself. A three-part rules explainer — created with creator Saeed Talks (@saeedtalks1) — walked players through the deck, the cards, and the play loop, dropped July 6, 2025 as a coordinated Reel series so the content rewarded followers who came back for the next part instead of catching one moment in the feed.

08 · The Takeaway

El Tekia didn't launch
a card game next to a
marketing campaign.
It launched a card game
inside
a fictional Egyptian
neighborhood.

By committing to the strategy's "beautiful chaos" promise — and weaving an illustrated Tekia universe through real-player content, lore, memes, and character builds — the brand earned permission to occupy a space no Egyptian card game has held before: a homegrown belonging brand, not a foreign-import substitute.

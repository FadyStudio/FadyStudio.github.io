01 — Problem

Ramadan ads have flattened Egypt's most flavorful month.

Ramadan is Egypt's Super Bowl of advertising — the noisiest, most expensive, most scrolled-past month of the year. Every dairy brand in the country shows up with the same playbook: a perfect family, a perfect plate, a celebrity reaching for the butter.

The audience has stopped watching.

Rotana, one of Egypt's leading dairy brands and a peak-season player on butter and ghee, needed to break out without buying its way out.

02 — Challenge

How can Rotana win Ramadan without buying a celebrity, a TV slot, or a perfect family?

i.
Interrupt the scroll Earn attention from an audience trained to ignore Ramadan ads.
ii.
Prove "natural" without lecturing Make a quality claim feel like culture, not a corporate line.
iii.
Do it on a fraction of the category's budget No celebrity. No influencer fee. No big TV buy. Whatever the campaign won, it had to win on craft.

03 — Target Audience

The Loud-Kitchen
Mom.

الام المصرية الاصيلة

The Egyptian mom whose own kitchen has more drama than any Ramadan TV ad.

She's not the woman the ads cast. She doesn't glide. She doesn't smile beatifically over a pot of molokhia. She's tasting from the spoon, yelling at the kids to set the table, halfway through three pots and one phone call. She is the funniest person in her own kitchen, and she has never seen herself in a Ramadan ad.

The category casts her as serene.
She lives loud.

Behind the insight

01

Watched every Egyptian butter and ghee Ramadan ad released between 2020 and 2025 — and counted the recurring beats. Every one of them used the same five.

02

Sat in 4 real Cairo and Delta kitchens during iftar prep and recorded what the kitchen actually sounds like.

03

Mapped Egypt's regional food personalities — Sa'idi, Fellahi, Alexandrian, Cairene — and realized the food itself has more accent than the actors in any commercial.

04

Doomscrolled the comment sections under last Ramadan's biggest dairy ads. The most common reply: "does anyone actually talk like this?"

Category audit · Kitchen ethnography · Regional mapping · Comment mining

04 — The Insight

Fake food has
no accent.

While other brands obsessed over perfect plating, they were ignoring the soul of the kitchen. Egypt isn't one flavor — it's a mix of distinct cultures with distinct voices. The food on the plate has more personality than the people in the ads.

05 — Strategy

Authentic voices, real connections.

Evict the humans. Hire the ingredients.

06 — The Campaign

Naturally
Gets Us.

We broke the fourth wall of the Egyptian kitchen. Personified the food. Gave every ingredient an accent, a backstory, an opinion — and let Rotana's 100% natural butter be the only ingredient with the credibility to settle the argument.

01

Hero copy

The Unsilent Kitchen.

The opening rule was: No Humans Allowed. Instead of a chef, the ingredients introduce themselves. We built a hierarchy — the jealous processed oils against the Royal Rotana Butter, cast as the posh New Zealander. Shot with cinematic precision usually reserved for high-end drama, turning a countertop into a battlefield of flavor.

02

Mini-copies

Kitchen Confessions.

We expanded the universe by giving ingredients an inner life. A bowl of foul has a mid-life crisis about its suhoor destiny. The molokhia gasps for the perfect sizzle. These weren't ads — they were character studies of the food Egyptians eat every day, told in the accents Egyptians actually speak.

03

Interactive

Fawazeer Riddles.

We revived the Ramadan tradition of riddles and gamified the campaign. Clever wordplay and double meanings dropped hints across the feed, forcing the audience to listen closely. Passive viewers became active debaters — and the comment sections turned into the ad's second screen.

01 The Problem

A pill that asks adults to admit something they've spent years pretending wasn't true.

Ramelact, by Egyptian pharma Rameda, is a lactase enzyme tablet that lets adults digest dairy without the bloat, the cramps, or the quiet bargaining you do with your stomach at 11 PM.

The product works. The audience is the problem. A meaningful share of Egyptian adults are functionally lactose intolerant — and have never named it. They've just learned to live with it.

You can't sell the cure to people who don't believe they have the condition.

02 The Challenge

How does Ramelact land in a category nobody's looking for — without making the audience feel diagnosed?

03 The Reframe

The conversation people have with dairy isn't medical. It's personal.

The first hypothesis was the obvious one: educate Egyptians on lactose intolerance. The symptoms. The science. The solution. The framing collapsed the second we tested it.

Egyptians don't take digestive discomfort to a doctor. They blame the meal, the night, the spice level — anyone but the dairy. Education would put Ramelact in a white coat. We needed it to sound like a friend at a dinner party.

So we stopped writing about a condition. We started writing about a relationship — the one every adult was already in, and already complaining about, in their own language.

04 Behind the Insight

Three rooms we sat in before we wrote a line.

Field

Read 200+ Egyptian threads on "وجع بطن بعد الأكل"

Not one person blamed the dairy. Everyone blamed the meal, the spice, the timing, the universe. The denial was the data.

Language

Mapped how the symptom gets rebranded in Amiya

"تقيلة عليّا," "مش متفقين," "بتعمل فيا حاجة" — the vocabulary was always relational. Never clinical. We borrowed every word back.

Category

Audited every lactose-intolerance brand worldwide

White coats. Blue gradients. Stock illustrations of bloated stomachs. Nobody, anywhere, was using emotional language for a digestive product. The lane was empty.

05 The Audience

The Quiet Negotiator.

Adults — foodies, everyday eaters, athletes — who've been in a private, ongoing arrangement with dairy for years. They'll have the cheesecake. They'll regret it by 11 PM. They've never said "lactose intolerant" out loud, but they've absolutely planned a whole day around what they ate the night before.

08 The Campaign
A campaign in three acts. The relationship arc.
الفراق مش قدرنا

A pitch built like a love story in reverse. Open with the breakup. Sit with the wreckage. Let Ramelact walk in as the reason you got back together — but the relationship logic kept evolving, and the toxic-ex framing became the brand's whole voice.

i.The Breakup

Teasers — "مكناش لبعض من الأول"

The relationship metaphor was front-loaded as visual confessions — long before the brand or product was named. A pizza on a table for one, candles lit, the line "نفسك في بيتزا؟ بس للأسف انتو مش لبعض." A cheesecake, a tissue, a hand reaching for something. Romance posters for breakups that hadn't happened yet.

Teaser · Pizza, Candles, Heartbreak Teaser · العلاقة دي اخرتها ايه؟
ii.The Reconciliation

Reveal — "اللي اتكسر يتصلح"

The product walks in as relationship advice. A man in a red sweater, glasses, fork twirling pasta — finally smiling. A woman holding ice cream like she just got an apology she'd been waiting for. The reveal didn't sell the pill. It sold the make-up.

Reveal · راميلاكت حل مشاكل المعدة
iii.The Group Chat

The Toxic-Relationship Reels — with Elly Ellaban

"لو اللبن بيوحشك في بعده وبيتعبك في قربه — يبقى الحل في راميلاكت."

The execution evolution that earned the campaign its biggest moment. Working with Egyptian comedian Elly Ellaban, the brand produced a series of Reels using Bushra's "انا خايف" — each one staging the toxic dynamic in a single beat: the love, the dread, the relief. This is where the strategy sharpened in public — from "rocky relationship" to "toxic ex."

The Static Universe — Dating, Heartbreak, Reconciliation

A series of art-directed stills extending the romance metaphor across the feed. Candlelit dinners. Breakup tableaux. Cheesecake-as-ex. Second-chance pasta nights. Each post reframed a different dairy product as a complicated lover the audience had absolutely been here with before.

09 The Takeaway

The campaign turned a clinical, low-search-volume category into an emotional one with cultural fluency.

And it earned Ramelact the right to keep speaking — not as a pill, but as the brand that finally said out loud what every Egyptian adult had been quietly negotiating with their fridge for years.

The Result It was a pitch — and the client said yes.
